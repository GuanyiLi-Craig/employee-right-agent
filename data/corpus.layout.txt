                   Employment Rights (Demonstration) Act 2026

                                2026 CHAPTER 14

An Act to make provision about employment rights, about pay, deductions and
working time, about protection from dismissal and detriment, about trade
unions and industrial relations, and about the enforcement of labour market
legislation; and for connected purposes.

                                                          [28th August 2026]

BE IT ENACTED by the King's most Excellent Majesty, by and with the advice
and consent of the Lords Spiritual and Temporal, and Commons, in this
present Parliament assembled, and by the authority of the same, as follows:—

                            Zero hours workers, etc

1     Right to guaranteed hours

    (1)  A worker has the right to a guaranteed hours offer where the
        conditions in subsection (2) are met.

    (2)  The employer must give the worker a written notice setting out a
        guaranteed hours offer before the end of the period of 14 days
        beginning with the day on which the duty under subsection (1)
        arises.

    (3)  The amount of compensation under this section must be such amount
        as the tribunal considers just and equitable, not exceeding 8 weeks'
        pay.

    (4)  In determining whether an employer has complied with subsection
        (3), a tribunal must have regard to any relevant code of practice
        and to the size and administrative resources of the employer's
        undertaking.

    (5)  This section does not apply in relation to a worker who is employed
        under a contract for a fixed term of less than 4 weeks, unless
        regulations provide otherwise.

    (6)  Where an employment tribunal finds a complaint under this section
        well-founded, it must make a declaration to that effect and may
        order the employer to pay compensation to the worker.

    (7)  This section applies whether or not the worker has been
        continuously employed for a period of 2 months ending with the
        relevant date.

    (8)  In the Employment Rights Act 1996, after section 27B insert—

              Guaranteed hours: further provision

              “27BA  Right to guaranteed hours

              (1)  An employer must secure that every worker to whom this
                  section applies is offered guaranteed hours in accordance
                  with this Chapter.
7      Employment Rights (Demonstration) Act 2026 (c. 14)
       Part 1 — Employment rights


              (2)  The duty under subsection (1) applies in relation to each
                  relevant reference period beginning after the coming into
                  force of this section.

              (3)  Regulations may make provision about the manner in which
                  guaranteed hours is to be offered, including provision
                  about the form of an offer and the period for acceptance.

              (4)  A worker may present a complaint to an employment
                  tribunal that an employer has failed to comply with
                  subsection (1).

              Supplementary

              “27BB  Right to guaranteed hours: supplementary

              (1)  In section 27BA, references to guaranteed hours include
                  references to an offer made by an agent of the employer.”


2     Guaranteed hours: number of hours to be offered

    (1)  Where a worker makes a request relating to the number of guaranteed
        hours, the employer must deal with the request in accordance with
        this section.

    (2)  Subsection (1) does not apply where the employer can show that the
        requirement was not reasonably practicable having regard to regular
        pattern.

    (3)  Regulations under subsection (2) may make provision about regular
        pattern, including provision conferring a discretion on an
        employment tribunal.

    (4)  The employer must keep a record of the number of guaranteed hours
        for a period of 2 years beginning with the end of the relevant
        reference period, and must make the record available to the worker
        on request.

    (5)  For the purposes of this section, "hours worked" means the period
        determined in accordance with regulations, and "regular pattern" is
        to be construed accordingly.

    (6)  The Secretary of State may by regulations specify the descriptions
        of hours worked to be taken into account for the purposes of
        subsection (5).

3     Guaranteed hours: exceptions for temporary need

    (1)  Where a worker makes a request relating to a temporary work
        exception, the employer must deal with the request in accordance
        with this section.

    (2)  Subsection (1) does not apply where the employer can show that the
        requirement was not reasonably practicable having regard to fixed
        term.

    (3)  Regulations under subsection (2) may make provision about fixed
        term, including provision conferring a discretion on an employment
        tribunal.

    (4)  The employer must keep a record of a temporary work exception for a
        period of 2 years beginning with the end of the relevant reference
        period, and must make the record available to the worker on request.

    (5)  For the purposes of this section, "temporary need" means the period
        determined in accordance with regulations, and "fixed term" is to be
        construed accordingly.

    (6)  The Secretary of State may by regulations specify the descriptions
        of temporary need to be taken into account for the purposes of
        subsection (5).


4     Duty to give reasonable notice of a shift

    (1)  Where a worker makes a request relating to notice of a shift, the
        employer must deal with the request in accordance with this section.

    (2)  The employer must give the worker a written notice setting out
        notice of a shift before the end of the period of 14 days beginning
        with the day on which the duty under subsection (1) arises.

    (3)  The amount of compensation under this section must be such amount
        as the tribunal considers just and equitable, not exceeding 4 weeks'
        pay.

    (4)  In determining whether an employer has complied with subsection
        (3), a tribunal must have regard to any relevant code of practice
        and to the size and administrative resources of the employer's
        undertaking.

    (5)  This section does not apply in relation to a worker who is employed
        under a contract for a fixed term of less than 8 weeks, unless

5     Right to payment for a cancelled shift

    (1)  This section applies where an employer proposes to make a decision
        about payment for a cancelled shift which affects a worker.

    (2)  Regulations under subsection (1) may make provision about
        cancellation payment, including provision conferring a discretion on
        an employment tribunal.

    (3)  The employer must keep a record of payment for a cancelled shift
        for a period of 6 years beginning with the end of the relevant
        reference period, and must make the record available to the worker
        on request.

    (4)  For the purposes of this section, "short notice cancellation" means
        the period determined in accordance with regulations, and
        "cancellation payment" is to be construed accordingly.

    (5)  The Secretary of State may by regulations specify the descriptions
        of short notice cancellation to be taken into account for the
        purposes of subsection (4).

    (6)  The threshold for the purposes of subsection (5) is £500, or such
        other amount as may be prescribed.


6     Zero hours workers: agency workers

    (1)  The Secretary of State must by regulations make provision about the
        application of this Chapter to agency workers, including provision
        about agency worker and hirer.

    (2)  The threshold for the purposes of subsection (1) is £1000, or such
        other amount as may be prescribed.

    (3)  The employer must give the worker a written notice setting out the
        application of this Chapter to agency workers before the end of the

    (5)  In determining whether an employer has complied with subsection

                                Flexible working

7     Right to request flexible working

    (1)  An employer must not, by reason of flexible working, treat a worker
        less favourably in relation to a flexible working request.

    (2)  Nothing in this section affects any right of the worker under a
        contract of employment, a collective agreement or any other
        enactment relating to working pattern.

    (3)  Where flexible working changes during the relevant reference
        period, the employer must review a flexible working request and
        notify the worker of the outcome of the review.

    (4)  Subsection (3) does not apply where the employer can show that the
        requirement was not reasonably practicable having regard to working
        pattern.

    (5)  Regulations under subsection (4) may make provision about working
        pattern, including provision conferring a discretion on an
        employment tribunal.

    (6)  The employer must keep a record of a flexible working request for a
        period of 6 years beginning with the end of the relevant reference
        period, and must make the record available to the worker on request.

    (7)  For the purposes of this section, "flexible working" means the
        period determined in accordance with regulations, and "working
        pattern" is to be construed accordingly.

12    Removal of the waiting period for statutory sick pay

    (1)  The Secretary of State must by regulations make provision about the
        waiting period, including provision about waiting period and period
        of incapacity.

    (2)  For the purposes of this section, "waiting period" means the period
        determined in accordance with regulations, and "period of
        incapacity" is to be construed accordingly.

    (3)  The Secretary of State may by regulations specify the descriptions
        of waiting period to be taken into account for the purposes of
        subsection (2).

    (4)  The threshold for the purposes of subsection (3) is £5000, or such
        other amount as may be prescribed.

    (5)  The employer must give the worker a written notice setting out the
        waiting period before the end of the period of 5 days beginning with
        the day on which the duty under subsection (1) arises.

    (6)  The amount of compensation under this section must be such amount
        as the tribunal considers just and equitable, not exceeding 26
        weeks' pay.
